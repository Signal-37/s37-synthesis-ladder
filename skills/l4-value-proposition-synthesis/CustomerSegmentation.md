# Customer Segmentation — When to Identify Target Customers Before Running This Skill

The skill builds the Customer Profile *from* L3 Insights — it does not require a pre-identified target customer list as input. But the Customer Profile is only coherent if it describes **one beneficiary trying to make one kind of progress**. A Customer Profile that tries to straddle two distinct customer types produces blurred jobs, diluted pains, and VPs too generic to be useful.

## The risk: segment blur

If L3 Insights were synthesized from mixed segments — participants with fundamentally different relationships to the product, brand, or job — the jobs inventory will contain multiple competing primary jobs. The High-Value Jobs screen can rank those jobs by importance, but it cannot tell you which segment each job belongs to. The resulting Customer Profile will be an average of the two customers, not a precise account of either.

**Test:** If your L3 Insights include jobs that are mutually exclusive or in tension (e.g., "win races" vs. "feel like a serious cyclist without racing"), you are looking at two customer types, not one.

## Two clean paths

**Option 1: Run the skill once per segment**
Build separate Customer Profiles and Value Maps for each distinct customer type. This is the right approach when the research data clearly supports multiple segments and the organization needs to understand the VP landscape across them. The outputs can be compared and prioritized at L5.

**Option 2: Identify a primary customer first**
Decide — before running the skill — which segment the brand most wants to win with. Synthesize for that one. Flag other segments as secondary profiles and note what VP work would be needed to serve them. This is the right approach when a strategic decision about who the primary customer is has already been made (or needs to be made before synthesis is useful).

## What this skill cannot do

The High-Value Jobs screen ranks jobs by importance, tangibility, satisfaction gap, and economic value — but it ranks *within* a defined customer scope. It cannot resolve a segmentation question the data leaves open. Feeding mixed-segment L3 Insights into the skill and expecting the screen to surface a clean primary job is asking the tool to do work that belongs upstream.

## Implication for multi-geography or multi-persona research

When L3 Insights span meaningfully different geographies, rider types, use contexts, or brand relationships, treat segmentation as a prerequisite step. Review the L3 frontmatter (participant types, segments) and decide:

- Are these insights describing one customer in different contexts?
- Or are they describing two (or more) customers who happen to use the same product?

If the latter: split before synthesizing, or accept that the VP output will require a segmentation caveat.
