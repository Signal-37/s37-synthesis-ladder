# Domain Scope Reference

Defines the domain-coupled vocabulary for L4 Archetypes. The synthesis machinery in `ArchetypeFormat.md` — the four behavioral spectrums, the fluidity principle, context triggers (situational and developmental), Key Indicators, LeCompte evidence typing, confidence/status declarations, and volume calibration — is universal. This file parameterizes only three layers: **who the archetype describes** (the behavioral actor being modeled), the **journey-domain vocabulary** (what "purchase contexts" or equivalent means in this domain), and the **design implications register** (which classes of decisions the archetypes inform).

Confirm the domain at the start of SynthesizeArchetypes (Step 1). If the L2 Themes carry a `domain:` field in their frontmatter, inherit it and confirm rather than re-asking. Carry the domain forward into the L4 output frontmatter.

The five domains match the rest of the ladder exactly.

> The journey-domain vocabulary below is a starting point, not a fence. Name the journey contexts the archetype actually activated in, even if they don't appear in the list.

---

## brand/design

**Who the archetype describes:** The customer or buyer — the person making purchase, loyalty, and repeat-engagement decisions in the category.

**Journey-domain vocabulary:** Frame selection · Component upgrade · Apparel & accessories · Race or event entry · Service & maintenance · Brand discovery (first contact) · Community participation · Gifting

**Naming mode recommendation:** Behavioral action labels (The Researcher, The Repeat Buyer, The Gift Buyer) as the default — direct, and easily adopted cross-functionally by brand, retail, and product teams. Plain descriptors (Buyers Who Want Advice) when what people want is what separates the archetypes. Either way the name must pass the first read (SKILL.md Core Concept 6).

**Design implications register:** Experience and communication design decisions — what the retail environment, digital touchpoint, brand communication, or community program must do to serve this archetype's behavioral mode. Informs: channel strategy, content register, product architecture, dealer programs, community activation, loyalty design.

## product/UX

**Who the archetype describes:** The user — the person accomplishing tasks with or through the product. Where buyer ≠ user, note the distinction in the archetype card's Journey Domains section.

**Journey-domain vocabulary:** Onboarding & first use · Core task completion · Advanced or power use · Integration with adjacent tools · Recovery from error or failure · Help-seeking & support · Upgrade or migration · Workflow automation

**Naming mode recommendation:** Behavioral action labels (The Planner, The Power User, The Workaround Builder, The Help Seeker) — direct and verb-driven, and easy for engineering, PM, and QA teams to apply in sprint reviews, design critiques, and accessibility audits.

**Design implications register:** Interaction and product design decisions — what the interface, flow, information architecture, or capability must do to serve this archetype's task mode. Informs: roadmap prioritization, feature scoping, UX pattern selection, information density calibration, notification and nudge strategy, accessibility design.

---

## organizational

**Who the archetype describes:** The employee, team member, or internal stakeholder — the person navigating the organizational system being studied.

**Journey-domain vocabulary:** Role onboarding · Day-to-day task execution · Cross-functional collaboration · Decision-making moments · Learning & skill development · Change adoption · Leadership interaction · Escalation & exception handling · Exit or transition

**Naming mode recommendation:** Behavioral action labels as the default (The Coordinator, The Self-Starter, The Rule Follower) — professional register, readable in leadership presentations. Use plain descriptors with care in organizational contexts: a name about what employees want can read as HR framing rather than behavioral analysis.

**Design implications register:** Organizational and process design decisions — what the workflow, tooling, team structure, or management practice must do to serve this archetype's behavioral mode. Informs: process redesign, tooling selection, role and decision-right definition, change management sequencing, training and enablement design, performance feedback design.

---

## policy/advocacy

**Who the archetype describes:** The constituent, citizen, beneficiary, or community member — the person the policy or program is designed to serve.

**Journey-domain vocabulary:** First point of contact with the system · Eligibility determination · Application or enrollment · Service receipt or program participation · Appeal or grievance · Re-engagement or renewal · Informal navigation (workarounds, peer guidance, community support networks)

**Naming mode recommendation:** Behavioral action labels when the deliverable is for a policy, government, or institutional audience — they read as behavioral analysis, not advocacy. Plain descriptors can work for advocacy organizations where the model will be used in community communication or public-facing materials, provided they describe what people want and do not judge it. A name that judges can read as prescriptive or normative rather than descriptive.

**Design implications register:** Service and program design decisions — what the intake process, service delivery model, eligibility structure, or frontline communication must do to serve this archetype's interaction mode. Informs: program design, access and eligibility rules, outreach and enrollment strategy, frontline staff training, service navigation tools, appeals process design.
